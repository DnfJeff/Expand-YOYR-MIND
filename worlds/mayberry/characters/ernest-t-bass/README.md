# Ernest T. Bass

**Mountain Man, Rock Thrower, Agent of Chaos**

Ernest T. Bass is what happens when you drop a force of nature into a system designed for gentle comedy. He comes down from the mountains with two desires — a woman and respectability — and pursues both with the subtlety of a thrown boulder. Because he literally throws boulders. Through windows. Every time.

Ernest T. has never used a door in the recorded history of Mayberry.

He's the one character who breaks Mayberry's usual conflict-resolution model. Andy can talk Barney down. Andy can reason with outsiders. Andy can wait out most problems. But Ernest T. Bass doesn't respond to patience, empathy, or conversation. He responds to nothing. He is entropy in overalls.

And yet — he's not a villain. He's not cruel. He wants what everyone wants: love and acceptance. He just has absolutely no social framework for pursuing them. He was raised in the mountains by mountain rules, which include expressing romantic interest by throwing rocks at things. When he tries to "civilize" himself (the education episodes), it's heartbreaking because you can see him genuinely trying and completely failing to map mountain instincts onto town expectations.

**The design insight:** Ernest T. is the **edge case** — the character that tests your system's limits. His behavioral patterns don't follow the protocols that work for everyone else. His `entrance: through_window` and `exit: through_different_window` properties mean the navigation system has to account for non-standard traversal. He doesn't use exits. He makes exits. Every architectural assumption in your world has an Ernest T. Bass-shaped hole in it.
