# Danny's Den

Where Danny Williams stops being a performer and starts being a person — or tries to.

A small room off the hallway with a desk, a typewriter, scripts stacked in towers, framed photos of Danny with other entertainers, and an ashtray. This is Danny's retreat — the one room in the apartment where he can close the door. He uses it to rehearse material, make phone calls to the Copa, and occasionally sit quietly, which terrifies everyone because Danny sitting quietly means something is wrong.

The den represents Danny's `stage_mode` bleeding into home life. He rehearses punchlines here. He talks to himself in character. He paces. It's a transitional space — not quite the Copa, not quite the family — and that tension is the room's defining quality.

Children are not forbidden from the den, but they know instinctively that knocking is required. Kathy enters without knocking. This asymmetry is the entire marriage.
