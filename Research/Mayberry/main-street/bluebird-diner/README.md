# Bluebird Diner

Mayberry's communal table — where everyone eats and nobody rushes.

Checkered tablecloths, a counter with stools, the smell of coffee and bacon from 6 AM until closing. The daily special is on a chalkboard by the door. You can sit at the counter for quick conversation or take a booth for longer business. Everyone's coffee mug has their name on it.

The Bluebird is the town's neutral ground. Floyd's is for gossip. The courthouse is for problems. The porch is for depth. The diner is for everything else — the routine transactions of daily life, conducted over pie and coffee that's been sitting a little too long but nobody minds.

Andy usually grabs coffee here before heading to the courthouse. It's the first social checkpoint of his day.

## The Phone

There's a phone at the Bluebird. It sits on the wall by the kitchen pass-through. When it rings and Juanita answers, it's just a waitress taking an order. When Barney calls, it's the most loaded phone line in Mayberry.

Barney cups his hand over the receiver. His voice drops. He laughs at things the audience can't hear. He glances around to make sure nobody's watching — in a town where everybody is always watching. The phone is where Barney's love triangle lives. Not at Thelma Lou's porch, not at the courthouse, not in any room where the relationship has to be real. On the phone, at the diner, with a woman the audience will never see.

Juanita works here. She's a waitress. She's good at her job. She laughs at Barney's jokes. Whether she knows she's the third point of a triangle or just thinks she has a friendly regular is one of the great unsolved questions of American television.

In MOOLLM terms, the diner's phone is a **remote advertisement** — a CARD.yml that satisfies `romance` and `validation` needs at a distance. Barney doesn't need to be in the room. He just needs the number. The phone turns the Bluebird into an extension of Barney's need system without him ever sitting down at the counter.
