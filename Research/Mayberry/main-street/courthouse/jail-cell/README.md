# Jail Cell

The most philosophically interesting room in Mayberry.

A cot, a sink, bars on the window, and a door that is never locked. The key hangs on a hook by the door — more tradition than function. Otis Campbell's initials are carved in the wall beside seven years of tally marks.

The jail cell is a **self-check-in system**. Otis walks himself here when he's had too much. He lets himself in. He sleeps it off. He lets himself out in the morning. No arrest, no booking, no paperwork, no judgment. The system works because Otis manages his own state transitions and the community accommodates rather than punishes.

Barney finds this arrangement personally offensive. Andy finds it entirely reasonable. The cell door is the most famous unlocked door in television history, and the fact that it stays unlocked is the most concise statement of Mayberry's governance philosophy: trust works better than locks.
