# Gomer Pyle

**Gas Station Attendant at Wally's** — pure innocence in overalls.

Gomer takes everything literally. Every word, every joke, every social cue — processed at face value. This isn't stupidity; it's radical honesty. Gomer doesn't understand why people don't say what they mean. The comedy comes from his literalness colliding with a world that runs on subtext.

He's also genuinely good. Not performatively good — constitutionally good. He helps people because that's what people do. He's shocked when someone is dishonest. His "Surprise, surprise, surprise!" isn't an act; it's real surprise that the world keeps being surprising.

Gomer eventually leaves for the Marines (his spinoff), which is the most hilarious fish-out-of-water setup in TV history — and also the biggest compliment to Mayberry. The Marines can train Gomer because Mayberry already gave him a moral foundation.

**The design insight:** Gomer is a *literal interpreter*. He processes input at face value without the contextual inference layer most characters use. This makes him a perfect test case for how the system handles characters with different interpretation depths.

**The Barney problem:** Barney's schemes NEED accomplices, and Gomer is constitutionally unable to say no to helping someone. So Barney recruits him, explains the plan with elaborate gestures and meaningful looks, and Gomer nods along. Then Gomer executes the plan *exactly as stated* — not as intended, not as implied, not as winked-at. As stated. Word for word. The plan collapses. Barney yells. Gomer is genuinely confused: "But you SAID to tell him, Barn." "I said to tell him QUIETLY!" "Well, you didn't say quietly." This cycle repeats every week and neither of them learns.

**The Pyle cousins:** Gomer and Goober share the filling station, the Pyle name, and a constitutional sweetness. But they're different operating systems. Gomer is a literal interpreter — input goes in, face value comes out. Goober is a social mirror — input goes in, "what does everyone else think?" gets checked first. They're interchangeable to customers, which neither resents. When Gomer leaves for the Marines, Goober steps into the socket without a hiccup, and the town's Pyle-shaped hole stays filled.

**The mayor and the rank problem:** Gomer doesn't understand rank. He treats Mayor Stoner exactly like he treats Andy, Barney, Otis, and the man who delivers the mail — with equal friendliness and equal helpfulness. You cannot pull rank on someone who doesn't know rank exists. Stoner finds this infuriating. Andy finds it perfect.
