# Goober Pyle

**Mechanic at Wally's Filling Station** — simple on the surface, savant underneath.

Goober is Gomer's cousin, and they share a constitutional sweetness. But where Gomer is a literal interpreter who processes the world at face value, Goober is a **domain expert** trapped in a general-purpose body. Put him in a social situation and he's lost — grinning, following the crowd, doing his Cary Grant impression for anyone who'll stand still. Put him under a car hood and he transforms. He can diagnose an engine knock by listening to it idle for three seconds. He can rebuild a transmission blindfolded. The mechanics of Goober's brain only engage when the problem is mechanical.

The comedy is people underestimating him because of the overalls and the beanie cap. The pathos is Goober knowing they do. He wants to be included with the guys at Floyd's, to be part of the inner circle, to matter beyond the garage. He usually gets there — Mayberry is too kind to leave anyone outside for long.

**The design insight:** Goober demonstrates **domain-specific competence** — a character whose intelligence is real but narrowly channeled. His CHARACTER.yml has a `special_skill: mechanical_intuition` that activates only in the garage. In every other room, his behavioral model runs on `follow_others`. The room determines which Goober you get.

**The Pyle cousins — two simplicity modes:** Goober and Gomer are the same family but different architectures. Gomer processes the world literally: what you say is what he hears. Goober processes it socially: what the group thinks is what he thinks. Both are "simple" characters, but the simplicity runs on different engines. Gomer's literalness makes him an accidental truth-teller. Goober's social mirroring makes him an accidental conformist. Together at the station they're indistinguishable to customers but deeply different to Andy and Barney, who've learned which Pyle to recruit for which situation. (Barney prefers Goober for schemes — at least Goober understands the *concept* of a secret plan, even if he can't keep one.)

**The Floyd's doorway:** Goober's social life is Floyd's barbershop. He leans in the doorway, sits in the extra chair, does his Cary Grant impression for anyone who'll stand still. Floyd tolerates it because Goober laughs at Floyd's stories, and in the barbershop economy, an audience is currency. The Cary Grant impression is terrible. Nobody tells him. Mayberry is too kind to take that from him.

**The mayor and the mechanics of democracy:** Goober votes. Goober talks at Floyd's about who he votes for. Mayor Stoner knows this. In a town this small, every ballot has a face, and Stoner treats Goober with careful friendliness — not because he respects Goober's opinion, but because he needs Goober's checkmark. Democracy at its most local.
