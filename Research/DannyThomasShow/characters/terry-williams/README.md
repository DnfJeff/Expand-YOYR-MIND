# Terry Williams

**The Daughter, Growing Up, Caught Between**

Terry is sixteen and navigating the specific challenge of being a teenager with a famous, dramatic, overprotective father. She's sensible in a household that runs on spectacle. She's responsible in a family that operates on impulse. She's the most normal person in the Williams apartment, and that normalcy constantly collides with Danny's theatrical baseline.

Terry wants independence — to date, to make her own choices, to be treated as an adult. Danny wants to protect her — from boys, from mistakes, from growing up. Every boyfriend introduction is a hostage negotiation. Every request for freedom is a constitutional debate. Kathy mediates, always, because without Kathy this relationship would be nothing but slammed doors and ceiling monologues.

What makes Terry interesting is her position between the two worlds. She's old enough to see Danny's drama for what it is (performance) but young enough to still need what's underneath it (love). She rolls her eyes and means it. She hugs him and means it. Both at the same time.

**The design insight:** Terry represents the **outgrowing-the-system** pattern — a character whose needs are evolving past what the current environment can satisfy. Her `independence` need is rising while Danny's `protectiveness` need pushes back. The system produces tension not from external events but from internal developmental pressure. Characters aren't static; their needs change over time.
