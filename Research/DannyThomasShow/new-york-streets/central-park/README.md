# Central Park

New York's breathing room — and the Williams family's closest approximation of a backyard.

Benches, paths, the reservoir glinting in the background, and pigeons that Rusty insists on feeding despite Danny's allergy to anything that requires standing still outdoors. Central Park is where the Williams family does the things families in houses do in their yards: play catch, walk together, sit and talk without the walls of the apartment defining the conversation.

The park functions as an **outdoor neutral zone** — similar to Mayberry's fishing hole but with a key difference. The fishing hole is solitude; Central Park is solitude surrounded by strangers. It's private in public. The ambient noise (kids, joggers, distant traffic) creates a white-noise privacy that the apartment's thin walls can't provide.

This is where Danny occasionally has a genuine quiet moment with one of his kids — a walk where the performer drops away and the father surfaces. The park's openness seems to open Danny up too.
