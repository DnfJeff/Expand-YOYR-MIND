# Building Lobby

The membrane between the Williams' private world and New York City.

A small lobby with a doorman who knows everyone by name, a row of brass mailboxes, and an elevator that takes forever. This is the space where visitors are first encountered — screened, announced, sometimes stalled ("Mr. Williams? Your uncle is here." "Which one?" "The loud one." "That doesn't narrow it down.").

The lobby is a **threshold room** — a transitional space that serves as the apartment's immune system. The doorman functions as a soft gatekeeper, giving the Williams family warning before disruption arrives at their door. It's the first line of the **visitor pattern**: arrival in the lobby triggers the cascade that brings chaos into the apartment.

In the Danny Thomas Show's centrifugal architecture, the lobby is the outer ring. New York enters here. By the time it reaches the living room, it's been filtered through at least one buffer.
